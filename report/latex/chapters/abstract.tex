\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
This report audits next-day market-movement classification (\emph{Fall}, \emph{Neutral}, \emph{Rise}) on the public NIFTY benchmark, which pairs SPY market context with financial headlines over 2010--2020. It asks how reported performance changes under different evaluation protocols and whether news adds predictive value beyond prices, including across volatility regimes and during the February--April 2020 stress window. Price sequences, frozen FinBERT headline representations, and three data-driven volatility regimes are evaluated using a fixed model family and ten random seeds.

The primary strict chronological evaluation (317 test rows) gives Macro-F1 $0.466$ for a price-only LSTM. Adding 768-dimensional FinBERT embeddings lowers the score to $0.416$ (difference $-0.050$, 95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.087,-0.012]$). A compact three-probability sentiment input scores $0.482$, but its difference from price-only is uncertain ($+0.016$, 95\% interval $[-0.017,0.049]$). The regime input does not yield a reliable overall gain. Looser protocols do not systematically increase scores; the clearest positive gaps occur for the regime-only model. Because the protocols use different test samples, these gaps cannot be attributed to leakage alone. The data audit indicates that each label corresponds to the next trading day's close-to-close return, while headline publication times are unavailable. Results are limited to this benchmark and do not establish trading profitability.
