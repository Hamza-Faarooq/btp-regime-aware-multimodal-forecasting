\section{Results}\label{ch:results}
\subsection{Reporting Conventions}
All numbers are means over ten seeds unless stated otherwise. Tables report test Macro-F1 under the strict protocol P3 unless a different protocol is named. The test set has 317 rows. M0 always predicts the training majority class; M0r guesses uniformly at random and is the appropriate floor for Macro-F1. Every value below is taken from the executed ten-seed run; nothing has been re-tuned after viewing results.

\figpage{fig_protocol_audit.png}{\textwidth}{Test Macro-F1 under the three protocols and the protocol gaps.}
For the models that use lagged prices and/or news (M1--M4) the looser protocols did \emph{not} score reliably higher than the strict one; most intervals include zero. The exception is the regime-only model M5, which scores higher under both looser protocols (+0.046 and +0.028). Regimes persist for weeks, so a random split places near neighbours of every test day in the training set, and fitting the clusters on all data lets test-period structure shape the regime labels. For M6 the strict protocol scored \emph{higher} than the random split. This reversal reinforces that the protocol gaps are model-dependent; because P1 changes the test sample across seeds, the observed gap also reflects sample composition and run-to-run variability, not leakage alone. Hypothesis H1 is therefore \textbf{not supported in general}, and supported only for the model that relies solely on a slowly varying state.

Four findings follow.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The FinBERT embedding hurts.} Adding it to the price model reduces Macro-F1 by 0.050 with an interval excluding zero; outside the stress window the reduction is 0.060.
\item \textbf{A low-capacity news input is about neutral.} Three sentiment scores give $+0.016$ overall with an interval including zero. The only interval excluding zero (Mid-vol, $+0.054$) is one of 24 comparisons and is treated as a hypothesis.
\item \textbf{The regime input adds little.} Adding the regime id to the price-plus-embedding model changes Macro-F1 by $+0.017$ (interval includes zero); the best news-plus-regime model (M3) does not beat price only.
\item \textbf{No sign of extra news value in the stress window.} In 1 February--30 April 2020 no model with news information differs reliably from price only. With only 50 rows the intervals are wide (about $\pm0.08$ to $0.10$), so moderate effects cannot be excluded.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Stress Window}\label{sec:stress}
Within the stress window the price-only model reaches 0.473, the random floor 0.316, and the embedding and regime models 0.448 and 0.450. None of the news-related differences is reliably non-zero (Table~\ref{tab:diffs}). Mean FinBERT sentiment in March 2020 is $-0.319$ against $-0.176$ for the remainder of the test set, so the headlines do register the shock, but this did not translate into a measurable gain in next-day classification.
